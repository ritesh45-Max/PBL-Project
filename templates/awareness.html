Digital Awareness

Learn to spot misleading content before you share it.

How Fake News Spreads

  • Emotional content such as fear, anger or surprise gets shared faster.
  • Messages forwarded in groups seem trustworthy because they come from people we know.
  • Algorithms reward engagement, so provocative posts reach more people.
  • Short clips and screenshots remove the original context.

How Deepfakes Are Created

  • AI models learn the face, voice and movements of a person from many samples.
  • The model then generates new footage of that person saying or doing something they never did.
  • Editing tools can also swap faces, change backgrounds or alter speech.
  • Quality keeps improving, so visual inspection alone is no longer reliable.

Identifying Suspicious Content

  • Headlines in ALL CAPS or with many exclamation marks.
  • Claims with no named source, date or link.
  • Pressure to share urgently before it is "deleted".
  • Images or videos with odd lighting, blurry edges, or unnatural blinking and lip movement.

Verifying News with Trusted Sources

  • Check the story on at least two reliable news outlets.
  • Go to the original source: official websites, press releases or documents.
  • Use reputable fact-checking organisations and reverse image or video search.
  • Pause before sharing. If you are unsure, do not forward it.
